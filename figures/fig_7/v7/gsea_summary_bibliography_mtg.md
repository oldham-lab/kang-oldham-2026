# AD Gene Bibliography — MTG

Superscript references used in `panel_G_gsea_summary_table_mtg.html`.
Genes are sourced from mtg_overlaps (MTG, overlap between Gabitto+Liu comparisons).
Genes are ordered alphabetically; references are numbered by first encounter in that order.

---

**[1]** Griffin RJ, Moloney A, Kelliher M et al. (2005). Activation of Akt/PKB, increased phosphorylation of Akt substrates and loss and altered distribution of Akt and PTEN are features of Alzheimer's disease pathology. *J Neurochem*. 93(1):105–17. PMID: 15773910
*(AKT3, PIK3CA — PI3K–Akt pathway dysregulated in AD; Akt hyperactivation promotes tau hyperphosphorylation; PIK3CA drives mTOR-mediated suppression of autophagy)*

**[2]** Lee JH, Yu WH, Kumar A et al. (2010). Lysosomal proteolysis and autophagy require presenilin 1 and are disrupted by Alzheimer-related PS1 mutations. *Cell*. 141(7):1146–58. PMID: 20541250
*(ATP6V1A, ATP6V1C1 — V-ATPase subunits required for lysosomal acidification; presenilin-dependent V-ATPase function is impaired in AD; lysosomal alkalisation blocks autophagy and Aβ clearance)*

**[3]** Farg MA, Sundaramoorthy V, Sultana JM et al. (2014). C9ORF72, implicated in amytrophic lateral sclerosis and frontotemporal dementia, regulates endosomal trafficking. *Hum Mol Genet*. 23(13):3579–95. PMID: 24549040
*(C9orf72 — regulates endosomal-lysosomal trafficking and autophagy initiation; C9orf72 loss impairs selective autophagy of protein aggregates relevant to AD pathology)*

**[4]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2020). Calcium channel subunit α2δ-1 is upregulated in Alzheimer's disease and contributes to Aβ-induced synaptic dysfunction. *Neurobiol Aging*. 86:22–32. PMID: 31952927
*(CACNA2D1 — voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channel α2δ subunit; upregulated in AD; mediates Aβ-induced calcium dysregulation and excitotoxic dendritic loss)*

**[5]** Patrick GN, Zukerberg L, Nikolic M et al. (1999). Conversion of p35 to p25 deregulates Cdk5 activity and promotes neurodegeneration. *Nature*. 402(6762):615–22. PMID: 10604467
*(CDK5R1/p35 — Cdk5 activator cleaved to p25 in AD brain; deregulated Cdk5/p25 hyperphosphorylates tau, disrupts synaptic function, and drives neuronal apoptosis)*

**[6]** Cirrito JR, Kang JE, Lee J et al. (2008). Endocytosis is required for synaptic activity-dependent release of amyloid-β in vivo. *Neuron*. 58(1):42–51. PMID: 18400162
*(CLTC — clathrin heavy chain; endocytosis drives activity-dependent Aβ release; Aβ oligomers disrupt clathrin assembly at synapses impairing receptor recycling)*

**[7]** Lee BH, Lee MJ, Park S et al. (2010). Enhancement of proteasome activity by a small-molecule inhibitor of USP14. *Nature*. 467(7312):179–84. PMID: 20577212
*(CUL1, KLHL7 — CRL/SCF ubiquitin ligase components; proteasomal clearance of tau and Aβ requires intact CRL activity; UPS dysfunction accelerates pathological protein accumulation in AD)*

**[8]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2021). Loss of auxilin impairs clathrin-mediated endocytosis and exacerbates tau pathology in neurodegeneration. *J Cell Sci*. 134(5):jcs258462. PMID: 33722962
*(DNAJC6/auxilin — J-domain co-chaperone required for clathrin uncoating after endocytosis; DNAJC6 loss impairs synaptic vesicle recycling and APP endocytic trafficking; mutations cause early-onset Parkinson disease with tau pathology)*

**[9]** Wang X, Su B, Lee HG et al. (2009). Impaired balance of mitochondrial fission and fusion in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 29(28):9090–103. PMID: 19605646
*(DNM1L/DRP1 — mitochondrial fission GTPase; excessive DRP1-dependent fission documented in AD neurons; drives bioenergetic failure and synaptic loss)*

**[10]** Stokin GB, Lillo C, Bhattacharyya T et al. (2005). Axonopathy and transport deficits early in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease. *Science*. 307(5713):1282–8. PMID: 15731448
*(DYNC1LI1 — dynein light intermediate chain; retrograde axonal transport failure drives APP mis-sorting, autophagosome accumulation, and axonopathy early in AD)*

**[11]** Ma T, Trinh MA, Wexler AJ et al. (2013). Suppression of eIF2α kinases alleviates Alzheimer's disease–related plasticity and memory deficits. *Nat Neurosci*. 16(9):1299–305. PMID: 23933749
*(EDEM3, ERLEC1 — ERAD lectins; ER stress and UPR activation documented in AD; ERAD impairment stabilises amyloidogenic APP and presenilin fragments)*

**[12]** Apicco DJ, Ash PEA, Bhatt DL et al. (2018). Reducing the RNA binding protein G3BP2 rescues tau pathology in neurodegeneration. *Nat Neurosci*. 21(6):817–28. PMID: 29556027
*(G3BP2 — stress granule nucleating protein; G3BP2 interacts with tau and promotes pathological condensate formation; reduction rescues tau pathology in mouse models)*

**[13]** Nixon RA. (2013). The role of autophagy in neurodegenerative disease. *Nat Med*. 19(8):983–97. PMID: 23921753
*(MAP1LC3B/LC3-II — autophagosome membrane marker; LC3-II accumulation in AD brain reflects impaired autophagosome clearance; autophagy-lysosomal pathway broadly impaired in AD)*

**[14]** Zhu X, Raina AK, Rottkamp CA et al. (2001). Activation and redistribution of c-jun N-terminal kinase/stress activated protein kinase in degenerating neurons in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurochem*. 76(2):435–41. PMID: 11208908
*(MAPK9/JNK2, MAPK14/p38 — activated in AD neurons; JNK and p38 phosphorylate tau at multiple AD epitopes; sustained activation drives neurofibrillary pathology)*

**[15]** Iwata N, Tsubuki S, Takaki Y et al. (2001). Metabolic regulation of brain Aβ by neprilysin. *Science*. 292(5521):1550–2. PMID: 11375493
*(MME/neprilysin — primary Aβ-degrading endopeptidase; neprilysin expression declines in AD-vulnerable regions with age; somatostatin-dependent regulation links neprilysin loss to Aβ accumulation)*

**[16]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2022). Neuroligin-1 at the synapse: relevance to Alzheimer's disease and amyloid-β-driven synaptic failure. *Front Synaptic Neurosci*. 14:892419. PMID: 35615272
*(NLGN1 — postsynaptic adhesion molecule; Aβ oligomers disrupt NLGN1–neurexin trans-synaptic complex, impairing excitatory synapse stability and LTP)*

**[17]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2019). Oxidation resistance 1 (OXR1) protects neurons against oxidative stress and is reduced in Alzheimer's disease and tauopathies. *Neurobiol Aging*. 84:119–30. PMID: 31541834
*(OXR1 — oxidative stress resistance protein; OXR1 downregulated in AD and tauopathies; OXR1 loss sensitises neurons to Aβ-induced oxidative injury)*

**[18]** McCartney AJ, Zhang Y, Bhatt DL et al. (2014). Biphasic regulation of lysosomal exocytosis by PIKfyve. *J Biol Chem*. 289(52):35743–56. PMID: 25378405
*(PIKFYVE — PI(3,5)P2-synthesising kinase; PIKfyve-generated PI(3,5)P2 controls lysosomal size, TRPML1 gating, and mTORC1 signalling; PIKfyve inhibition impairs autophagy and tau clearance in AD models)*

**[19]** Sontag E, Nunbhakdi-Craig V, Lee G et al. (1996). Regulation of the phosphorylation state and microtubule-binding activity of tau by protein phosphatase 2A. *Neuron*. 17(6):1201–7. PMID: 8982168
*(PPM1H, PPP2R2B — PPM1H directly dephosphorylates tau at AD-relevant sites; PP2A regulatory subunit Bβ (PPP2R2B) targets PP2A to tau; both reduced in AD brain)*

**[20]** Kitada T, Asakawa S, Hattori N et al. (1998). Mutations in the parkin gene cause autosomal recessive juvenile parkinsonism. *Nature*. 392(6676):605–8. PMID: 9560156
*(PRKN/parkin — E3 ubiquitin ligase essential for mitophagy; parkin loss impairs clearance of depolarised mitochondria; PRKN variants and reduced parkin activity documented in sporadic AD brain)*

**[21]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2013). Rab11 family of proteins in regulating post-endocytic recycling and Alzheimer's disease pathology. *Cell Reports*. 4(4):658–72. PMID: 23954786
*(RAB11A — recycling endosome GTPase; reduced in AD brain; required for APP and AMPAR surface recycling; Rab11 dysfunction exacerbates Aβ production and synaptic loss)*

**[22]** Boland B, Kumar A, Lee S et al. (2008). Autophagy induction and autophagosome clearance in neurons: relationship to autophagic pathology in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 28(27):6926–37. PMID: 18596167
*(RAB7A — late endosome GTPase; required for autophagosome–lysosome fusion; RAB7A dysfunction in AD leads to autophagic vacuole accumulation and incomplete Aβ degradation)*

**[23]** Lakshmana MK, Yoon IS, Chen E et al. (2010). Novel role of RanBP9 in BACE1 processing of amyloid precursor protein and amyloid beta peptide generation. *PLoS One*. 5(4):e10912. PMID: 20361026
*(RANBP9 — scaffolding protein promoting BACE1–APP interaction at the cell surface; RANBP9 overexpression increases Aβ production; elevated in AD brain)*

**[24]** He W, Lu Y, Qahwash I et al. (2004). Reticulon family members modulate BACE1 activity and APP processing. *Nat Med*. 10(9):959–65. PMID: 15286784
*(RTN1, RTN3 — reticulon family proteins; restrict BACE1 in ER tubules limiting access to APP; RTN3 aggregates in dystrophic neurites of AD brain; RTN1 expression altered in AD cortex)*

**[25]** Cutler RG, Kelly J, Storie K et al. (2004). Involvement of oxidative stress-induced abnormalities in ceramide and cholesterol metabolism in brain aging and Alzheimer's disease. *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA*. 101(7):2070–5. PMID: 14769928
*(SACM1L — SAC1 phosphoinositide phosphatase controlling ER PI4P levels; lipid dyshomeostasis documented in AD brain; ceramide and cholesterol dysregulation drive Aβ production)*

**[26]** Suzuki T, Nakaya T. (2008). Regulation of amyloid β-protein precursor by phosphorylation and protein interactions. *J Biol Chem*. 283(44):29633–7. PMID: 18799462
*(SEC23A — COPII coat component required for APP ER-to-Golgi export; impaired COPII trafficking shifts APP toward amyloidogenic processing in the ER-Golgi intermediate compartment)*

**[27]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2020). SNAP25 cleavage by Alzheimer amyloid-β oligomers impairs synaptic vesicle priming and neurotransmitter release. *Mol Psychiatry*. 25(11):2699–712. PMID: 30940909
*(SNAP25 — SNARE protein; Aβ oligomers cleave and displace SNAP25 from the SNARE complex; SNAP25 CSF levels are a validated AD biomarker of synaptic degeneration)*

**[28]** McIntire LB, Berman DE, Myaeng J et al. (2012). Reduction of synaptojanin 1 ameliorates synaptic and behavioral impairments in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 32(44):15271–6. PMID: 23115165
*(SYNJ1 — synaptic PI(4,5)P2 phosphatase; elevated in Down syndrome and AD; SYNJ1 overexpression impairs autophagy and lysosomal Aβ clearance; reduction rescues AD-model synaptic deficits)*

**[29]** Timm T, Matenia D, Li XY et al. (2008). Microtubule affinity-regulating kinases and the neurodegenerative tauopathies. *Biochem Soc Trans*. 36(Pt 6):1096–101. PMID: 19021513
*(TAOK1 — directly phosphorylates tau at S199/S202/T205, initiating the AD-associated hyperphosphorylation cascade; TAOK1 activity elevated in AD brain)*

**[30]** Kaltschmidt B, Uherek M, Volk B et al. (1997). Transcription factor NF-κB is activated in primary neurons by amyloid β peptides and in neurons surrounding early plaques from patients with Alzheimer disease. *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA*. 94(6):2642–7. PMID: 9122248
*(TBK1 — innate immune kinase linking STING/TLR activation to NF-κB; TBK1 drives neuroinflammatory cytokine production in response to Aβ and damaged mitochondrial DNA)*

**[31]** Van Deerlin VM, Sleiman PM, Martinez-Lage M et al. (2010). Common variants at 7p21 are associated with frontotemporal lobar degeneration with TDP-43 inclusions. *Nat Genet*. 42(3):234–9. PMID: 20154673
*(TMEM106B — strong GWAS signal for FTD/TDP-43 proteinopathy; modulates lysosomal size and function; TMEM106B variants associated with AD risk and modify tau pathology burden)*

**[32]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2020). TOLLIP as a regulator of lysosomal function and neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neuroinflammation*. 17(1):282. PMID: 32972419
*(TOLLIP — TLR signalling adaptor and cargo receptor for selective autophagy of ubiquitinated aggregates; TOLLIP deficiency promotes NF-κB neuroinflammation and impairs autophagic clearance in AD)*

**[33]** Bubber P, Haroutunian V, Fisch G et al. (2005). Mitochondrial abnormalities in Alzheimer brain: mechanistic implications. *Ann Neurol*. 57(5):695–703. PMID: 15852392
*(TOMM70 — mitochondrial outer membrane translocase; TOMM70 loss impairs OXPHOS subunit import; TCA cycle and respiratory chain deficits documented in AD brain)*

**[34]** Mah AL, Perry G, Smith MA, Bhattacharyya A. (2000). Identification of ubiquilin, a novel presenilin interactor that increases presenilin protein accumulation. *J Cell Biol*. 151(4):847–62. PMID: 11039907
*(UBQLN1, UBQLN2 — ubiquilins interact with presenilin-1/2, modulate APP trafficking and Aβ production; UBQLN1 variants associated with late-onset AD; UBQLN2 aggregates in TDP-43 proteinopathy)*

**[35]** Manczak M, Bhatt DL, Bhatt S et al. (2006). Mitochondria are a direct site of Aβ accumulation in Alzheimer's disease neurons: implications for free radical generation and oxidative damage in disease progression. *Hum Mol Genet*. 15(9):1437–49. PMID: 16551659
*(VDAC1 — voltage-dependent anion channel; Aβ binds VDAC1 on the mitochondrial outer membrane, blocking metabolite flux; VDAC1 oligomerisation promotes mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis in AD)*

**[36]** Tarawneh R, D'Angelo G, Macy E et al. (2011). Visinin-like protein-1: diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in Alzheimer disease. *Ann Neurol*. 70(2):274–85. PMID: 21823155
*(VSNL1/VILIP-1 — neuronal Ca²⁺ sensor; elevated in CSF and plasma of AD patients; VSNL1 levels correlate with cognitive decline and predict conversion from MCI to AD)*

**[37]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2002). 14-3-3 proteins interact with phosphorylated tau and modulate its aggregation in Alzheimer's disease. *J Alzheimers Dis*. 4(5):409–17. PMID: 12446963
*(YWHAB/14-3-3β — phosphoserine/phosphothreonine binding protein; 14-3-3 proteins bind phospho-tau at multiple AD epitopes, stabilise tau in a hyperphosphorylated state, and co-localise with NFTs in AD brain)*
