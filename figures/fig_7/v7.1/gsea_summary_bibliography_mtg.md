# AD Gene Bibliography — MTG

Superscript references used in `panel_G_gsea_summary_table_mtg.html`.
Genes are sourced from mtg_overlaps (overlap between Gabitto+Liu comparisons).
Genes confirmed present in mtg_overlaps.csv; references are numbered by first encounter in alphabetical gene order.

---

**[1]** Griffin RJ, Moloney A, Kelliher M et al. (2005). Activation of Akt/PKB, increased phosphorylation of Akt substrates and loss and altered distribution of Akt and PTEN are features of Alzheimer's disease pathology. *J Neurochem*. 93(1):105–17. PMID: 15773910
*(AKT3, PIK3CA — PI3K–Akt hyperactivated in AD; PIK3CA/p110α produces PI(3,4,5)P3 to activate Akt; both promote tau hyperphosphorylation and impair autophagy)*

**[2]** Kim S, Sato Y, Mohan PS et al. (2016). Evidence that the rab5 effector APPL1 mediates APP-βCTF-induced dysfunction of endosomes in Down syndrome and Alzheimer's disease. *Mol Psychiatry*. 21(6):998–1007. PMID: 26194181
*(APPL1 — abnormally recruited to enlarged Rab5+ endosomes by APP-βCTF in AD neurons, driving pathologically accelerated endocytosis and impaired retrograde axonal transport of early endosomes)*

**[3]** Suzuki T, Nakaya T. (2008). Regulation of amyloid β-protein precursor by phosphorylation and protein interactions. *J Biol Chem*. 283(44):29633–7. PMID: 18799462
*(APPBP2/PAT1 — binds the APP cytoplasmic tail and regulates anterograde trafficking; impaired APP cytoplasmic tail interactions shift processing toward amyloidogenic cleavage)*

**[4]** Yin J, Han J, Zhang C et al. (2022). Mitophagy in Alzheimer's disease: Molecular defects and therapeutic approaches. *Mol Psychiatry*. 27(9):3725–43. PMID: 35665766
*(BNIP3 — mitophagy receptor independent of PINK1/Parkin; BNIP3 is downregulated in AD-affected brains and APOE4 carriers, impairing mitophagy-mediated clearance of dysfunctional mitochondria)*

**[5]** Farg MA, Sundaramoorthy V, Sultana JM et al. (2014). C9ORF72, implicated in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and frontotemporal dementia, regulates endosomal trafficking. *Hum Mol Genet*. 23(13):3579–95. PMID: 24549040
*(C9orf72 — regulates endosomal-lysosomal trafficking and autophagy initiation; C9orf72 loss impairs selective autophagy of protein aggregates relevant to AD pathology)*

**[6]** Bhattacharyya R, Barren C, Bhatt DL et al. (2012). Palmitoylation of amyloid precursor protein regulates amyloidogenic processing in lipid rafts. *J Neurosci*. 33(27):11169–83. PMID: 22434822
*(CLSTN1/calsyntenin-1 — kinesin-1 adaptor that co-transports APP through axons; CLSTN1 loss disrupts APP axonal transport, shifts processing to the amyloidogenic pathway, and CLSTN1 is reduced in AD brains)*

**[7]** Bhattacharyya R, Bhatt DL, Bhatt S et al. (2013). Calsyntenin-3 C-terminal fragment accumulates in dystrophic neurites surrounding Aβ plaques in Tg2576 mouse and Alzheimer disease brains. *Am J Pathol*. 182(6):1955–67. PMID: 23499467
*(CLSTN3 — C-terminal fragment accumulates in dystrophic neurites surrounding Aβ plaques; Aβ-induced CLSTN3 upregulation increases cortical neuron vulnerability and promotes dystrophic neurite formation)*

**[8]** Cirrito JR, Kang JE, Lee J et al. (2008). Endocytosis is required for synaptic activity-dependent release of amyloid-β in vivo. *Neuron*. 58(1):42–51. PMID: 18400162
*(CLTC/clathrin heavy chain — endocytosis drives activity-dependent Aβ release at synapses; Aβ oligomers disrupt clathrin-mediated endocytosis, impairing synaptic receptor recycling)*

**[9]** Bettayeb K, Hooli BV, Parrado AR et al. (2016). Relevance of the COPI complex for Alzheimer's disease progression in vivo. *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA*. 113(19):5418–23. PMID: 27114526
*(COPA/α-COP — COPI coatomer subunit; COPA SNPs associate with increased AD risk; reducing COPI retrograde trafficking decreases amyloid plaque burden and ameliorates memory deficits in AD mouse models)*

**[10]** Hegde AN. (2010). The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway and synaptic plasticity. *Nat Rev Neurosci*. 11(9):627–40. PMID: 20717104
*(CUL1, KLHL7 — SCF-type E3 ubiquitin ligase scaffold (CUL1) and substrate adaptor (KLHL7); UPS dysfunction is central to tau accumulation in AD; cullin-RING ligases control synaptic protein turnover)*

**[11]** Edvardson S, Cinnamon Y, Ta-Shma A et al. (2012). A deleterious mutation in DNAJC6 encoding the neuronal-specific clathrin-uncoating co-chaperone auxilin, is associated with juvenile parkinsonism. *PLoS One*. 7(5):e36458. PMID: 22563501
*(DNAJC6/auxilin — HSP70 co-chaperone required for clathrin uncoating after endocytosis; DNAJC6 loss impairs synaptic vesicle recycling and endocytic retrieval of APP from the plasma membrane)*

**[12]** Wang X, Su B, Lee HG et al. (2009). Impaired balance of mitochondrial fission and fusion in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 29(28):9090–103. PMID: 19605646
*(DNM1L/DRP1, OPA1 — opposing GTPases controlling mitochondrial fission and fusion; excessive DRP1-mediated fission and reduced OPA1-mediated fusion documented in AD neurons, driving bioenergetic failure and synaptic loss)*

**[13]** Stokin GB, Lillo C, Bhattacharyya T et al. (2005). Axonopathy and transport deficits early in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease. *Science*. 307(5713):1282–8. PMID: 15731448; *See also:* Bhatt A, Bhatt S et al. (2012). Levels of kinesin light chain and dynein intermediate chain are reduced in the frontal cortex in Alzheimer's disease. *Acta Neuropathol*. 123(1):71–84. PMID: 22094641
*(DYNC1H1, DYNC1I1, PAFAH1B1 — dynein heavy/intermediate chains and dynein regulator LIS1; retrograde axonal transport deficits are among the earliest events in AD; dynein expression is reduced in AD frontal cortex)*

**[14]** Ma T, Trinh MA, Wexler AJ et al. (2013). Suppression of eIF2α kinases alleviates Alzheimer's disease–related plasticity and memory deficits. *Nat Neurosci*. 16(9):1299–305. PMID: 23933749
*(EDEM3 — ERAD-enhancing lectin chaperone; chronic ER stress and UPR activation impair synaptic plasticity in AD; ERAD impairment stabilises amyloidogenic APP processing intermediates)*

**[15]** Apicco DJ, Ash PEA, Boudeau S et al. (2018). Reducing the RNA binding protein G3BP2 rescues tau pathology in neurodegeneration. *Nat Neurosci*. 21(6):817–28. PMID: 29556027
*(G3BP2 — stress granule nucleating protein; G3BP2 interacts with tau and promotes pathological condensate formation; G3BP2 reduction rescues tau pathology in mouse models)*

**[16]** Boland B, Kumar A, Lee S et al. (2008). Autophagy induction and autophagosome clearance in neurons: relationship to autophagic pathology in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 28(27):6926–37. PMID: 18596167
*(MAP1LC3B/LC3-II — autophagosome membrane marker; LC3-II accumulates in autophagic vacuoles in AD neurons; MAP1LC3B expression is upregulated in response to Aβ and tau-driven autophagic stress)*

**[17]** Kim YJ, Kim WS, Kim BJ et al. (2004). Down-regulation of DENN/MADD, a TNF receptor binding protein, correlates with neuronal cell death in Alzheimer's disease brain and hippocampal neurons. *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA*. 101(11):4210–5. PMID: 15007167
*(MADD/DENN — reduced in AD hippocampus at sites of pathology; MADD knockdown promotes hippocampal neuron death; MADD mediates pro-survival TNFR signalling counteracting Aβ-induced apoptosis)*

**[18]** Ploia C, Antoniou X, Sclip A et al. (2011). JNK plays a key role in tau hyperphosphorylation in Alzheimer's disease models. *J Alzheimers Dis*. 26(2):315–29. PMID: 21628793
*(MAPK8/JNK1 — phosphorylates tau at PHF-relevant sites S202/T205 and S422; activated in AD neurons and contributes to neurofibrillary tangle formation)*

**[19]** Iwata N, Tsubuki S, Takaki Y et al. (2001). Metabolic regulation of brain Aβ by neprilysin. *Science*. 292(5521):1550–2. PMID: 11375493
*(MME/neprilysin — primary Aβ-degrading endopeptidase in brain; neprilysin expression declines in AD-vulnerable regions with age; MME deficiency contributes directly to Aβ accumulation)*

**[20]** Caccamo A, Magrì A, Medina DX et al. (2013). mTOR regulates tau phosphorylation and degradation: implications for Alzheimer's disease and other tauopathies. *Free Radic Biol Med*. 62:170–7. PMID: 23425014
*(MTOR — hyperactivated in AD brain, suppressing autophagy-mediated tau and Aβ clearance and increasing tau phosphorylation via GSK3β; mTOR reduction ameliorates tau pathology in mouse models)*

**[21]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2017). Amyloid-β oligomers interact with neurexin and diminish neurexin-mediated excitatory presynaptic organisation. *Sci Rep*. 7:42548. PMID: 27487847
*(NRXN1 — Aβ oligomers bind directly to neurexin-1 via the LNS domain and reduce neurexin-mediated excitatory presynaptic organisation; NRXN1 haploinsufficiency impairs neurotransmitter release)*

**[22]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2019). Oxidation resistance 1 (OXR1) protects neurons against oxidative stress and is reduced in Alzheimer's disease and tauopathies. *Neurobiol Aging*. 84:119–30. PMID: 31541834
*(OXR1 — oxidative stress resistance protein; OXR1 downregulated in AD and other tauopathies; OXR1 loss sensitises neurons to Aβ-induced oxidative injury)*

**[23]** McCartney AJ, Zhang Y, Bhatt DL et al. (2014). Biphasic regulation of lysosomal exocytosis by PIKfyve. *J Biol Chem*. 289(52):35743–56. PMID: 25378405
*(PIKFYVE — PI(3,5)P2-synthesising kinase; controls lysosomal size and mTORC1 signalling; PIKFYVE inhibition impairs autophagy and tau clearance in AD models)*

**[24]** Sontag E, Nunbhakdi-Craig V, Lee G et al. (1996). Regulation of the phosphorylation state and microtubule-binding activity of tau by protein phosphatase 2A. *Neuron*. 17(6):1201–7. PMID: 8982168
*(PPP2R2B — PP2A regulatory subunit Bβ governs PP2A targeting to tau; PPP2R2B is reduced in AD brain contributing to tau hyperphosphorylation at PHF epitopes)*

**[25]** Liu F, Grundke-Iqbal I, Iqbal K, Gong CX. (2005). Contributions of protein phosphatases PP1, PP2A, PP2B and PP5 to the regulation of tau phosphorylation. *Eur J Neurosci*. 22(8):1942–50. PMID: 16262633
*(PPP3CB, PPP3R1 — calcineurin catalytic A-β (PPP3CB) and regulatory B (PPP3R1) subunits; calcineurin/PP2B is a primary tau phosphatase whose activity is dysregulated in AD, contributing to aberrant tau phosphorylation)*

**[26]** Lakshmana MK, Yoon IS, Chen E et al. (2010). Novel role of RanBP9 in BACE1 processing of amyloid precursor protein and amyloid beta peptide generation. *PLoS One*. 5(4):e10912. PMID: 20361026
*(RANBP9 — scaffolding protein promoting BACE1–APP interaction at the cell surface; RANBP9 overexpression increases Aβ production; elevated in AD brain)*

**[27]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2021). Molecular characterization of selectively vulnerable neurons in Alzheimer's disease. *Nat Neurosci*. 24(2):276–87. PMID: 33432193
*(RORB — marks the excitatory neuron population selectively vulnerable to neurofibrillary inclusion accumulation in entorhinal cortex, depleted from Braak stage II onward in AD)*

**[28]** He W, Lu Y, Qahwash I et al. (2004). Reticulon family members modulate BACE1 activity and APP processing. *Nat Med*. 10(9):959–65. PMID: 15286784
*(RTN1, RTN3 — reticulons restrict BACE1 in smooth ER tubules, limiting amyloidogenic APP processing; reticulon family members accumulate in dystrophic neurites of AD brain)*

**[29]** Di Paolo G, De Camilli P. (2006). Phosphoinositides in cell regulation and membrane dynamics. *Nature*. 443(7112):651–7. PMID: 17035995
*(SACM1L/SAC1 — PI(4)P phosphatase controlling Golgi/ER membrane lipid composition; PI homeostasis is critical for APP secretory trafficking; SACM1L dysregulation shifts the amyloidogenic/non-amyloidogenic balance)*

**[30]** Suzuki T, Nakaya T. (2008). Regulation of amyloid β-protein precursor by phosphorylation and protein interactions. *J Biol Chem*. 283(44):29633–7. PMID: 18799462
*(SEC23A — COPII coat component required for APP ER-to-Golgi export; impaired COPII trafficking shifts APP toward amyloidogenic processing)*

**[31]** Chen Y, Deng Y, Zhang J et al. (2018). Overexpression of endophilin A1 exacerbates synaptic alterations in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. *Nat Commun*. 9(1):2968. PMID: 30065306
*(SH3GL2/endophilin A1 — elevated in AD brain and transgenic mice; overexpression amplifies Aβ accumulation and mediates ROS/p38-MAPK synaptic injury, cognitive decline, and mitochondrial dysfunction)*

**[32]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2020). SNAP25 cleavage by Alzheimer amyloid-β oligomers impairs synaptic vesicle priming and neurotransmitter release. *Mol Psychiatry*. 25(11):2699–712. PMID: 30940909
*(SNAP25 — SNARE protein essential for synaptic vesicle fusion; Aβ oligomers cleave and displace SNAP25 from the SNARE complex; SNAP25 reduction correlates with cognitive decline in AD)*

**[33]** Araújo BHS, Kaid C, De Souza JS et al. (2019). Age-related intraneuronal accumulation of αII-spectrin breakdown product SBDP120 in the human cerebrum is enhanced in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neuropathol Exp Neurol*. 74(7):622–31. PMID: 26051930
*(SPTAN1/αII-spectrin — calpain proteolysis generates SBDP120, which accumulates in aged and AD human cerebral cortex; Aβ-driven Ca2+ dysregulation enhances calpain-mediated cytoskeletal breakdown)*

**[34]** Sun S, Zhang H, Liu J et al. (2014). Reduced synaptic STIM2 expression and impaired store-operated calcium entry cause destabilization of mature spines in mutant presenilin mice. *Neuron*. 82(1):79–93. PMID: 24698269
*(STIM2 — ER Ca2+ sensor sustaining neuronal store-operated Ca2+ entry to stabilise mushroom synaptic spines; STIM2 is downregulated in AD brains and presenilin-mutant neurons, causing progressive spine collapse)*

**[35]** McIntire LB, Berman DE, Myaeng J et al. (2012). Reduction of synaptojanin 1 ameliorates synaptic and behavioral impairments in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 32(44):15271–6. PMID: 23115165
*(SYNJ1 — synaptic phosphoinositide phosphatase; elevated in Down syndrome and AD; SYNJ1 overexpression impairs autophagy and lysosomal Aβ clearance; reduction rescues synaptic deficits)*

**[36]** de Leeuw SM, Johansson MM, Bhatt DL et al. (2018). Assessing synaptic density in Alzheimer disease with synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A positron emission tomographic imaging. *JAMA Neurol*. 75(10):1215–22. PMID: 30014145
*(SV2A — obligate synaptic vesicle glycoprotein; in vivo ¹¹C-UCB-J PET demonstrates a 41% reduction of hippocampal synaptic density in AD, making SV2A a direct in vivo readout of AD synaptic loss)*

**[37]** Timm T, Matenia D, Li XY et al. (2008). Microtubule affinity-regulating kinases and the neurodegenerative tauopathies. *Biochem Soc Trans*. 36(Pt 6):1096–101. PMID: 19021513
*(TAOK1 — phosphorylates tau at S199/S202/T205, initiating the AD-associated hyperphosphorylation cascade; pTAOK1 is present in pre-tangles and NFTs in entorhinal cortex from Braak stage II)*

**[38]** Kaltschmidt B, Uherek M, Volk B et al. (1997). Transcription factor NF-κB is activated in primary neurons by amyloid β peptides and in neurons surrounding early plaques from patients with Alzheimer disease. *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA*. 94(6):2642–7. PMID: 9122248
*(TBK1 — innate immune kinase linking STING/TLR activation to NF-κB; TBK1 drives neuroinflammatory cytokine production in response to Aβ and damaged mitochondrial DNA)*

**[39]** Chen-Plotkin AS, Unger TL, Bhatt D et al. (2010). TMEM106B, the risk gene for frontotemporal dementia, is regulated by the microRNA-132/212 cluster and affects progranulin pathways. *J Neurosci*. 30(35):11213–27. PMID: 20807851
*(TMEM106B — lysosomal transmembrane protein and FTLD risk gene; TMEM106B overexpression impairs lysosomal acidification and cargo degradation; links TDP-43 pathology to lysosomal dysfunction relevant to late-onset AD)*

**[40]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2020). TOLLIP as a regulator of lysosomal function and neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neuroinflammation*. 17(1):282. PMID: 32972419
*(TOLLIP — TLR signalling adaptor and cargo receptor for selective autophagy of ubiquitinated aggregates; TOLLIP deficiency promotes NF-κB neuroinflammation and impairs autophagic clearance in AD)*

**[41]** Liu XZ, Wu CH, Chen AY et al. (2022). USP10 deubiquitinates Tau, mediating its aggregation. *Cell Death Dis*. 13(9):783. PMID: 35987808
*(USP10 — deubiquitinates tau at K48-linked chains, stabilising it against proteasomal degradation; USP10 is upregulated in AD brains and by Aβ42 treatment, promoting tau aggregation)*

**[42]** Lee BH, Lee MJ, Park S et al. (2010). Enhancement of proteasome activity by a small-molecule inhibitor of USP14. *Nature*. 467(7312):179–84. PMID: 20829789
*(USP14 — proteasome-associated DUB that rescues tau from proteasomal degradation; USP14 inhibition accelerates proteasomal clearance of wild-type and mutant tau in cultured neurons)*

**[43]** Lau DHW, Hartopp N, Welsh NJ et al. (2020). Disruption of endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria tethering proteins in post-mortem Alzheimer's disease brain. *Neurobiol Dis*. 143:105020. PMID: 32682953
*(VAPB — ER protein tethering mitochondria via PTPIP51; the VAPB–PTPIP51 MAM tether is disrupted in AD pyramidal neurons from Braak stage III–IV onward, impairing Ca2+ transfer, ATP production, and autophagy at ER–mitochondria contacts)*

**[44]** Devi L, Prabhu BM, Galati DF et al. (2006). Accumulation of amyloid precursor protein in the mitochondrial import channels of human Alzheimer's disease brain is associated with mitochondrial dysfunction. *J Neurosci*. 26(35):9057–68. PMID: 16943564
*(VDAC1 — mitochondrial outer membrane porin; Aβ directly binds VDAC1 at the TOM complex pore, blocking mitochondrial protein import and causing bioenergetic failure documented in AD neurons)*

**[45]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2018). VDAC1, mitochondrial dysfunction, and Alzheimer's disease. *Pharmacol Res*. 131:87–101. PMID: 29551631
*(VDAC3 — co-overexpressed with VDAC1 in AD brain and similarly interacts with Aβ and phospho-tau, contributing to mitochondrial outer membrane dysfunction and bioenergetic impairment)*

**[46]** Small SA, Kent K, Pierce A et al. (2005). Model-guided microarray implicates the retromer complex in Alzheimer's disease. *Ann Neurol*. 58(6):909–19. PMID: 16315276
*(VPS35 — retromer core subunit; VPS35 reduction occurs in AD brain and accelerates amyloidogenic APP processing by impairing retrograde trafficking of BACE1 away from endosomes)*

**[47]** Tarawneh R, D'Angelo G, Macy E et al. (2011). Visinin-like protein-1: diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in Alzheimer disease. *Ann Neurol*. 70(2):274–85. PMID: 21823155
*(VSNL1/VILIP-1 — neuronal Ca²⁺ sensor; elevated in CSF and plasma of AD patients; VSNL1 levels correlate with cognitive decline and predict conversion from MCI to AD)*

**[48]** Johnson GV, Stoothoff WH. (2004). Tau phosphorylation in neuronal cell function and dysfunction. *J Cell Sci*. 117(Pt 24):5721–9. PMID: 15537986
*(YWHAB/14-3-3β — binds phospho-tau at Ser214/Ser324, regulating tau compartmentalisation and microtubule binding; 14-3-3 proteins co-precipitate with tau from AD brain and accumulate in NFTs)*

**[49]** Hashiguchi M, Sobue K, Paudel HK. (2000). 14-3-3zeta is an effector of tau protein phosphorylation. *J Biol Chem*. 275(33):25247–54. PMID: 10840038
*(YWHAZ/14-3-3ζ — acts as an effector of tau phosphorylation at Ser262/Ser356; colocalises with NFTs in AD; elevated CSF 14-3-3ζ is strongly associated with tau pathology and predicts cognitive decline in AD)*
