# Bibliography: AD gene database — CTRLmods_mtg overlaps
# Genes covered: ATG7, DNM1L, KIF5A, KIF5C, LRRK2, MAP1LC3B, MAPK8, MTOR,
#                NDUFS1, NDUFS4, NMNAT2, OPA1, PICALM, PPP3CB, PSMA5, PSMD5,
#                PSMD11, PTEN, SORL1, STIM2, SYNJ1, UBQLN1, VDAC1, VPS35

**[1]** Komatsu M et al. (2007). Essential role for autophagy protein Atg7 in the maintenance of axonal homeostasis and the prevention of axonal degeneration. Proc Natl Acad Sci. 104(36):14489-14494. PMID: 17726112
*(ATG7 — neuron-specific deletion of ATG7 in mice causes axonal swellings, ubiquitinated protein accumulation, and progressive neurodegeneration resembling AD pathology, establishing constitutive autophagy as essential for neuronal homeostasis)*

**[2]** Wang X et al. (2009). Amyloid-beta overproduction causes abnormal mitochondrial dynamics via differential modulation of mitochondrial fission/fusion proteins. J Neurosci. 29(28):9090-9103. PMID: 19605645
*(DNM1L, OPA1 — Aβ overproduction in AD neurons decreases fusion proteins MFN1 and OPA1 while increasing fission mediator DRP1/DNM1L, resulting in mitochondrial fragmentation, bioenergetic failure, and synaptic loss)*

**[3]** Kamal A et al. (2001). Kinesin-mediated axonal transport of a membrane compartment containing β-secretase and GPI-anchored proteins is associated with Alzheimer pathology. Cell. 107(6):787-797. PMID: 11747814
*(KIF5A, KIF5C — conventional kinesin (KIF5 heavy chains) mediates fast anterograde axonal transport of APP together with its processing machinery including BACE1; impaired transport leads to APP retention and enhanced Aβ generation)*

**[4]** Plowey ED et al. (2008). Role of autophagy in G2019S-LRRK2-associated neurite shortening in differentiated SH-SY5Y cells. J Neurochem. 105(3):1048-1056. PMID: 18384652
*(LRRK2 — the AD/PD-relevant kinase LRRK2 phosphorylates multiple autophagy and endolysosomal regulators; pathogenic LRRK2 mutations impair autophagic flux and cause neurite retraction, while LRRK2 also modulates tau phosphorylation via downstream kinase cascades)*

**[5]** Nixon RA et al. (2005). Extensive involvement of autophagy in Alzheimer disease: an immuno-electron microscopy study. J Neuropathol Exp Neurol. 64(2):113-122. PMID: 15751225
*(MAP1LC3B — LC3/MAP1LC3B accumulates in autophagic vacuoles that are massively expanded in dystrophic AD neurites, indicating a bottleneck at the autophagosome-lysosome fusion step)*

**[6]** Zhu X et al. (2001). Differential activation of neuronal ERK, JNK/SAPK and p38 in Alzheimer disease: the "two hit" hypothesis. Mech Ageing Dev. 123(1):39-46. PMID: 11640950
*(MAPK8 — JNK1/MAPK8 is aberrantly activated in AD neurofibrillary tangle-bearing neurons; JNK phosphorylates tau at Ser202/Thr205 and contributes to progressive tau pathology and neuronal death in AD brain)*

**[7]** Spilman P et al. (2010). Inhibition of mTOR by rapamycin abolishes cognitive deficits and reduces amyloid-beta levels in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. PLoS ONE. 5(4):e9979. PMID: 20376348
*(MTOR — mTOR hyperactivation in AD suppresses autophagy and promotes tau and Aβ accumulation; rapamycin treatment restores autophagy, reduces Aβ42 load, and rescues cognitive deficits in AD mouse models)*

**[8]** Bubber P et al. (2005). Mitochondrial abnormalities in Alzheimer brain: mechanistic implications. Ann Neurol. 57(5):695-703. PMID: 15761824
*(NDUFS1, NDUFS4 — activities of NADH dehydrogenase (Complex I, containing NDUFS1 and NDUFS4) are selectively reduced in AD brain regions with highest neurofibrillary tangle burden, linking Complex I dysfunction to bioenergetic failure in AD)*

**[9]** Ljungberg MC et al. (2012). CREB-activity and NMNAT2 transcription are down-regulated prior to neurodegeneration, while NMNAT2 over-expression is neuroprotective, in a mouse model of human tauopathy. Hum Mol Genet. 21(2):251-267. PMID: 22003072
*(NMNAT2 — NMNAT2, a labile axonal survival factor synthesising NAD+, declines early in tau-driven neurodegeneration; restoring NMNAT2 levels suppresses tau aggregation and protects axons from degeneration, with NMNAT2 also reduced in human AD brain)*

**[10]** Harold D et al. (2009). Genome-wide association study identifies variants at CLU and PICALM associated with Alzheimer's disease. Nat Genet. 41(10):1088-1093. PMID: 19734902
*(PICALM — PICALM is a genome-wide significant AD susceptibility locus; PICALM regulates clathrin-mediated endocytosis of APP and modulates autophagic clearance of Aβ, placing it at the intersection of two key AD pathogenic pathways)*

**[11]** Wu HY et al. (2010). Amyloid β induces the morphological neurodegenerative triad of spine loss, dendritic simplification, and neuritic dystrophies through calcineurin activation. J Neurosci. 30(8):2636-2649. PMID: 20164346
*(PPP3CB — Aβ oligomers activate calcineurin (PP2B, whose regulatory subunit is encoded by PPP3CB), which dephosphorylates cofilin and activates downstream pathways leading to dendritic spine loss and synaptic failure characteristic of early AD)*

**[12]** Keller JN et al. (2000). Impaired proteasome function in Alzheimer's disease. J Neurochem. 75(1):436-439. PMID: 10841806
*(PSMA5, PSMD5, PSMD11 — chymotrypsin-like activity of the 20S proteasome (PSMA5) and 19S regulatory subunit activities (PSMD5, PSMD11) are all significantly reduced in AD hippocampus, impairing clearance of oxidised and ubiquitinated proteins)*

**[13]** Griffin RJ et al. (2005). Activation of Akt/PKB, increased phosphorylation of Akt substrates and loss and altered distribution of Akt and PTEN are features of Alzheimer's disease pathology. J Neurochem. 93(1):105-117. PMID: 15773910
*(PTEN — PTEN protein is mislocalized and functionally reduced in AD neurons, leading to unchecked PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling that suppresses autophagy and promotes tau hyperphosphorylation via GSK3β disinhibition)*

**[14]** Rogaeva E et al. (2007). The neuronal sortilin-related receptor SORL1 is genetically associated with Alzheimer disease. Nat Genet. 39(2):168-177. PMID: 17220890
*(SORL1 — SORL1/SorLA is a neuronal sorting receptor that diverts APP away from amyloidogenic compartments toward retromer-mediated recycling; SORL1 expression is reduced in AD brain and multiple genetic variants in SORL1 confer AD risk)*

**[15]** Zhang H et al. (2015). Neuronal store-operated calcium entry and mushroom spine loss in amyloid precursor protein knock-in mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. J Neurosci. 35(39):13275-13286. PMID: 26424879
*(STIM2 — STIM2, the ER Ca2+ sensor that activates SOCE to maintain resting spine Ca2+, is reduced in AD brain and AD mouse models; STIM2 downregulation causes mushroom spine loss and synaptic dysfunction reversible by SOCE restoration)*

**[16]** Cossec JC et al. (2012). Trisomy for synaptojanin1 in Down syndrome is functionally linked to the enlargement of early endosomes. Hum Mol Genet. 21(14):3156-3172. PMID: 22505588
*(SYNJ1 — triplication of the chromosome-21 gene SYNJ1 in Down syndrome causes excess PI(4,5)P2 at endocytic membranes, enlarges early endosomes, and accelerates APP processing to Aβ, mechanistically linking SYNJ1 dosage to early-onset AD in trisomy 21)*

**[17]** Mah AL et al. (2000). Identification of ubiquilin, a novel presenilin interactor that increases presenilin protein accumulation. J Cell Biol. 151(4):847-862. PMID: 10952998
*(UBQLN1 — ubiquilin-1 directly binds presenilin-1 and -2 and stabilises their expression; UBQLN1 also acts as a UBL-UBA shuttle factor linking ubiquitinated substrates to the proteasome, and its dysfunction impairs both APP/presenilin stability and proteasomal clearance in AD)*

**[18]** Anandatheerthavarada HK et al. (2003). Mitochondrial targeting and a novel transmembrane arrest of Alzheimer's amyloid precursor protein impairs mitochondrial function as a new mechanism of neuronal death. J Cell Biol. 161(6):1079-1090. PMID: 14691135
*(VDAC1 — full-length APP accumulates in the mitochondrial import channel where it physically interacts with VDAC1, inhibiting mitochondrial import of cytochrome c oxidase subunits and reducing Complex IV activity and ΔΨm in AD neurons)*

**[19]** Small SA et al. (2005). Model-guided microarray implicates the retromer complex in Alzheimer's disease. Ann Neurol. 58(6):909-919. PMID: 16315276
*(VPS35 — retromer-complex component VPS35 is selectively reduced in the entorhinal cortex of AD patients; retromer deficiency impairs endosome-to-Golgi retrieval of SorLA/SORL1 and thereby increases APP residence time in endosomes where BACE1 is active, increasing Aβ production)*
