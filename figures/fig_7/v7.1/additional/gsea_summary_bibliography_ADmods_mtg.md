# Bibliography: AD gene database — ADmods_mtg overlaps
# Genes covered: APP, ATF6, ATG5, BECN1, CDK5, CHMP2B, DAPK1, DNM1L, DYRK1A,
#                GSK3B, ITPR1, KIF5A, KIF5C, MAP1LC3B, MAP3K12, MAPK8, MFN1,
#                MTOR, NDUFS1, NDUFS4, NMNAT2, OPA1, PICALM, PPP3CB, PRNP,
#                PSMA5, PSMD5, PSMD8, PTEN, RAB5A, STIM2, SYNJ1, UBQLN1,
#                VDAC1, VDAC2, VPS35, WDFY3

**[1]** Hardy J, Selkoe DJ (2002). The amyloid hypothesis of Alzheimer's disease: progress and problems on the road to therapeutics. Science. 297(5580):353-356. PMID: 12130773
*(APP — APP encodes the amyloid precursor protein whose sequential cleavage by β- and γ-secretase generates Aβ42; the amyloid cascade hypothesis places APP processing at the apex of AD pathogenesis)*

**[2]** Hoozemans JJM et al. (2005). The unfolded protein response is activated in Alzheimer's disease. Acta Neuropathol. 110(2):165-172. PMID: 15731894
*(ATF6 — all three arms of the unfolded protein response (ATF6, PERK/eIF2α, IRE1/XBP1) are activated in AD brain, with ATF6 target genes elevated in tangle-bearing neurons, linking ER proteostatic failure to AD neurodegeneration)*

**[3]** Hara T et al. (2006). Suppression of basal autophagy in neural cells causes neurodegenerative disease in mice. Nature. 441(7095):885-889. PMID: 16625204
*(ATG5 — conditional neuronal deletion of ATG5 blocks autophagosome formation, causing progressive neurodegeneration with ubiquitinated inclusions, directly implicating core autophagy machinery in neuronal maintenance relevant to AD)*

**[4]** Pickford F et al. (2008). The autophagy-related protein beclin 1 shows reduced expression in early Alzheimer disease and regulates amyloid β accumulation in mice. J Clin Invest. 118(6):2190-2199. PMID: 18497889
*(BECN1 — beclin-1 is reduced in early AD cortex; heterozygous deletion in mice increases Aβ accumulation and impairs lysosomal clearance of APP C-terminal fragments)*

**[5]** Cruz JC et al. (2003). Aberrant Cdk5 activation by p25 triggers pathological events leading to neurodegeneration and neurofibrillary tangles. Neuron. 40(3):471-483. PMID: 14642273
*(CDK5 — proteolytic conversion of the CDK5 activator p35 to p25 by calpain causes prolonged CDK5 hyperactivation in AD neurons, leading to hyperphosphorylation of tau at multiple Alzheimer epitopes and neurofibrillary tangle formation)*

**[6]** Skibinski G et al. (2005). Mutations in the endosomal ESCRTIII-complex subunit CHMP2B in frontotemporal dementia. Nat Genet. 37(8):806-808. PMID: 16041373
*(CHMP2B — dominant missense mutations in CHMP2B, an ESCRT-III component required for endosomal sorting and autophagosome maturation, cause frontotemporal dementia with tau/ubiquitin pathology sharing features with AD)*

**[7]** Zalckvar E et al. (2009). DAP-kinase-mediated phosphorylation on the BH3 domain of beclin 1 promotes dissociation of beclin 1 from Bcl-XL and induction of autophagy. EMBO Rep. 10(3):285-292. PMID: 19180116
*(DAPK1 — DAPK1 phosphorylates beclin-1 at Thr119 to release it from Bcl-XL and activate autophagy; DAPK1 is also elevated in AD brain and phosphorylates NR2B to mediate Aβ-induced excitotoxicity)*

**[8]** Wang X et al. (2009). Amyloid-beta overproduction causes abnormal mitochondrial dynamics via differential modulation of mitochondrial fission/fusion proteins. J Neurosci. 29(28):9090-9103. PMID: 19605645
*(DNM1L, MFN1, OPA1 — Aβ overproduction in AD neurons decreases fusion proteins MFN1 and OPA1 while increasing fission mediator DRP1/DNM1L, resulting in mitochondrial fragmentation, bioenergetic failure, and synaptic loss)*

**[9]** Ryoo SR et al. (2007). DYRK1A-mediated hyperphosphorylation of Tau: a functional link between Down syndrome and Alzheimer disease. J Biol Chem. 282(48):34850-34857. PMID: 17906291
*(DYRK1A — DYRK1A directly phosphorylates tau at Thr212, priming it for subsequent GSK3β phosphorylation; DYRK1A overexpression in Down syndrome and AD brain contributes to neurofibrillary tangle formation)*

**[10]** Phiel CJ et al. (2003). GSK-3α regulates production of Alzheimer's disease amyloid-β peptides. Nature. 423(6938):435-439. PMID: 12761548
*(GSK3B — GSK3 isoforms regulate both APP processing to generate Aβ and tau phosphorylation at multiple AD-relevant epitopes; GSK3 inhibition reduces Aβ42 production and tau hyperphosphorylation in vivo)*

**[11]** Stutzmann GE et al. (2004). Dysregulated IP3 signaling in cortical neurons of knock-in mice expressing an Alzheimer's-linked mutation in presenilin1 results in exaggerated Ca2+ signals and altered membrane excitability. Neuron. 42(4):499-510. PMID: 15047074
*(ITPR1 — PS1 FAD mutations potentiate IP3R-mediated ER Ca2+ release, causing supranormal cytosolic Ca2+ transients that contribute to synaptic dysfunction and neurodegeneration in AD)*

**[12]** Kamal A et al. (2001). Kinesin-mediated axonal transport of a membrane compartment containing β-secretase and GPI-anchored proteins is associated with Alzheimer pathology. Cell. 107(6):787-797. PMID: 11747814
*(KIF5A, KIF5C — conventional kinesin (KIF5 heavy chains) mediates fast anterograde axonal transport of APP together with its processing machinery including BACE1; impaired transport leads to APP retention and enhanced Aβ generation)*

**[13]** Nixon RA et al. (2005). Extensive involvement of autophagy in Alzheimer disease: an immuno-electron microscopy study. J Neuropathol Exp Neurol. 64(2):113-122. PMID: 15751225
*(MAP1LC3B — LC3/MAP1LC3B accumulates in autophagic vacuoles that are massively expanded in dystrophic AD neurites, indicating a bottleneck at the autophagosome-lysosome fusion step)*

**[14]** Le Pichon CE et al. (2017). Loss of dual leucine zipper kinase signaling is protective in animal models of neurodegenerative disease. Sci Transl Med. 9(403):eaag0394. PMID: 28794283
*(MAP3K12 — MAP3K12/DLK is a critical mediator of the neuronal stress-response kinase cascade activated downstream of Aβ and tau pathology; genetic or pharmacological DLK inhibition reduces synaptic loss and neurodegeneration in AD models)*

**[15]** Zhu X et al. (2001). Differential activation of neuronal ERK, JNK/SAPK and p38 in Alzheimer disease: the "two hit" hypothesis. Mech Ageing Dev. 123(1):39-46. PMID: 11640950
*(MAPK8 — JNK1/MAPK8 is aberrantly activated in AD neurofibrillary tangle-bearing neurons; JNK phosphorylates tau at Ser202/Thr205 and contributes to progressive tau pathology and neuronal death in AD brain)*

**[16]** Spilman P et al. (2010). Inhibition of mTOR by rapamycin abolishes cognitive deficits and reduces amyloid-beta levels in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. PLoS ONE. 5(4):e9979. PMID: 20376348
*(MTOR — mTOR hyperactivation in AD suppresses autophagy and promotes tau and Aβ accumulation; rapamycin treatment restores autophagy, reduces Aβ42 load, and rescues cognitive deficits in AD mouse models)*

**[17]** Bubber P et al. (2005). Mitochondrial abnormalities in Alzheimer brain: mechanistic implications. Ann Neurol. 57(5):695-703. PMID: 15761824
*(NDUFS1, NDUFS4 — activities of NADH dehydrogenase (Complex I) and other mitochondrial enzyme complexes are selectively reduced in AD brain regions with highest neurofibrillary tangle burden, linking NDUFS1/NDUFS4-containing Complex I to bioenergetic failure in AD)*

**[18]** Ljungberg MC et al. (2012). CREB-activity and NMNAT2 transcription are down-regulated prior to neurodegeneration, while NMNAT2 over-expression is neuroprotective, in a mouse model of human tauopathy. Hum Mol Genet. 21(2):251-267. PMID: 22003072
*(NMNAT2 — NMNAT2, a labile axonal survival factor synthesising NAD+, declines early in tau-driven neurodegeneration; restoring NMNAT2 levels suppresses tau aggregation and protects axons from degeneration, with NMNAT2 also reduced in human AD brain)*

**[19]** Harold D et al. (2009). Genome-wide association study identifies variants at CLU and PICALM associated with Alzheimer's disease. Nat Genet. 41(10):1088-1093. PMID: 19734902
*(PICALM — PICALM is a genome-wide significant AD susceptibility locus; PICALM regulates clathrin-mediated endocytosis of APP and modulates autophagic clearance of Aβ, placing it at the intersection of two key AD pathogenic pathways)*

**[20]** Wu HY et al. (2010). Amyloid β induces the morphological neurodegenerative triad of spine loss, dendritic simplification, and neuritic dystrophies through calcineurin activation. J Neurosci. 30(8):2636-2649. PMID: 20164346
*(PPP3CB — Aβ oligomers activate calcineurin (PP2B, whose regulatory subunit is encoded by PPP3CB), which dephosphorylates cofilin and activates downstream pathways leading to dendritic spine loss and synaptic failure characteristic of early AD)*

**[21]** Lauren J et al. (2009). Cellular prion protein mediates impairment of synaptic plasticity by amyloid-β oligomers. Nature. 457(7233):1128-1132. PMID: 19242574
*(PRNP — cellular prion protein (PrPC) acts as a high-affinity receptor for Aβ oligomers at the synapse; PrP–Aβ binding activates mGluR5 and Fyn kinase, mediating tau phosphorylation and the synaptic toxicity of soluble Aβ)*

**[22]** Keller JN et al. (2000). Impaired proteasome function in Alzheimer's disease. J Neurochem. 75(1):436-439. PMID: 10841806
*(PSMA5, PSMD5, PSMD8 — chymotrypsin-like, trypsin-like, and peptidyl-glutamyl-peptide hydrolase activities of the 20S proteasome (PSMA subunits) and 19S regulatory particle (PSMD subunits) are all significantly reduced in AD hippocampus, impairing clearance of oxidised and ubiquitinated proteins)*

**[23]** Griffin RJ et al. (2005). Activation of Akt/PKB, increased phosphorylation of Akt substrates and loss and altered distribution of Akt and PTEN are features of Alzheimer's disease pathology. J Neurochem. 93(1):105-117. PMID: 15773910
*(PTEN — PTEN protein is mislocalized and functionally reduced in AD neurons, leading to unchecked PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling that suppresses autophagy and promotes tau hyperphosphorylation via GSK3β disinhibition)*

**[24]** Ginsberg SD et al. (2010). Upregulation of select rab GTPases in cholinergic basal forebrain neurons in mild cognitive impairment and Alzheimer's disease. J Alzheimers Dis. 22(3):771-781. PMID: 20847419
*(RAB5A — RAB5A is selectively upregulated in vulnerable cholinergic neurons in MCI and AD, consistent with enlarged early endosomes and increased endocytic flux that accelerates Aβ generation from APP in the endosomal compartment)*

**[25]** Zhang H et al. (2015). Neuronal store-operated calcium entry and mushroom spine loss in amyloid precursor protein knock-in mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. J Neurosci. 35(39):13275-13286. PMID: 26424879
*(STIM2 — STIM2, the ER Ca2+ sensor that activates store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE) to maintain resting spine Ca2+, is reduced in AD brain and AD mouse models; STIM2 downregulation causes mushroom spine loss and synaptic dysfunction reversible by SOCE restoration)*

**[26]** Cossec JC et al. (2012). Trisomy for synaptojanin1 in Down syndrome is functionally linked to the enlargement of early endosomes. Hum Mol Genet. 21(14):3156-3172. PMID: 22505588
*(SYNJ1 — triplication of the chromosome-21 gene SYNJ1 in Down syndrome causes excess PI(4,5)P2 at endocytic membranes, enlarges early endosomes, and accelerates APP processing to Aβ, mechanistically linking SYNJ1 dosage to early-onset AD in trisomy 21)*

**[27]** Mah AL et al. (2000). Identification of ubiquilin, a novel presenilin interactor that increases presenilin protein accumulation. J Cell Biol. 151(4):847-862. PMID: 10952998
*(UBQLN1 — ubiquilin-1 directly binds presenilin-1 and -2 and stabilises their expression; UBQLN1 also acts as a UBL-UBA shuttle factor linking ubiquitinated substrates to the proteasome, and its dysfunction impairs both APP/presenilin stability and proteasomal clearance in AD)*

**[28]** Anandatheerthavarada HK et al. (2003). Mitochondrial targeting and a novel transmembrane arrest of Alzheimer's amyloid precursor protein impairs mitochondrial function as a new mechanism of neuronal death. J Cell Biol. 161(6):1079-1090. PMID: 14691135
*(VDAC1, VDAC2 — full-length APP accumulates in the mitochondrial import channel where it physically interacts with VDAC1/VDAC2, inhibiting mitochondrial import of cytochrome c oxidase subunits and reducing Complex IV activity and ΔΨm in AD neurons)*

**[29]** Small SA et al. (2005). Model-guided microarray implicates the retromer complex in Alzheimer's disease. Ann Neurol. 58(6):909-919. PMID: 16315276
*(VPS35 — retromer-complex component VPS35 is selectively reduced in the entorhinal cortex of AD patients; retromer deficiency impairs endosome-to-Golgi retrieval of SorLA/SORL1 and thereby increases APP residence time in endosomes where BACE1 is active, increasing Aβ production)*

**[30]** Filimonenko M et al. (2010). The selective macroautophagic degradation of aggregated proteins requires the PI3P-binding protein Alfy. Mol Cell. 38(2):265-279. PMID: 20417604
*(WDFY3 — WDFY3/Alfy is the PI3P-binding scaffold that recruits the ATG5-12-16L1 conjugation complex to ubiquitinated protein aggregates for selective macroautophagic clearance; its loss causes accumulation of polyubiquitinated inclusions resembling those in AD and other neurodegenerative diseases)*
