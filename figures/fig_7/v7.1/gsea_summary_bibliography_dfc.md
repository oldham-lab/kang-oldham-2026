# AD Gene Bibliography — DFC

Superscript references used in `panel_G_gsea_summary_table.html`.
Genes are sourced from dfc_overlaps (overlap between Gabitto+Liu comparisons).
Genes confirmed present in dfc_overlaps.csv; references are numbered by first encounter in alphabetical gene order.

---

**[1]** Griffin RJ, Moloney A, Kelliher M et al. (2005). Activation of Akt/PKB, increased phosphorylation of Akt substrates and loss and altered distribution of Akt and PTEN are features of Alzheimer's disease pathology. *J Neurochem*. 93(1):105–17. PMID: 15773910
*(AKT3 — Akt/PKB hyperactivated in AD brain; aberrant PI3K–Akt signalling promotes tau hyperphosphorylation and impairs autophagy)*

**[2]** Kim S, Sato Y, Mohan PS et al. (2016). Evidence that the rab5 effector APPL1 mediates APP-βCTF-induced dysfunction of endosomes in Down syndrome and Alzheimer's disease. *Mol Psychiatry*. 21(6):998–1007. PMID: 26194181
*(APPL1 — APPL1/Rab5 effector is abnormally recruited to enlarged Rab5+ endosomes by APP-βCTF in AD neurons, driving pathologically accelerated endocytosis and impaired retrograde axonal transport)*

**[3]** Suzuki T, Nakaya T. (2008). Regulation of amyloid β-protein precursor by phosphorylation and protein interactions. *J Biol Chem*. 283(44):29633–7. PMID: 18799462
*(APPBP2, SEC23A — APPBP2/PAT1 binds the APP cytoplasmic tail and regulates anterograde trafficking; SEC23A drives COPII-mediated APP ER-to-Golgi export; impaired secretory trafficking shifts APP toward amyloidogenic processing)*

**[4]** Bettayeb K, Hooli BV, Parrado AR et al. (2016). Relevance of the COPI complex for Alzheimer's disease progression in vivo. *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA*. 113(19):5418–23. PMID: 27114526
*(COPA — α-COP COPI coatomer subunit; COPA SNPs are associated with increased AD risk and reducing COPI retrograde trafficking decreases amyloid plaque burden and ameliorates memory deficits in AD mouse models)*

**[5]** Stokin GB, Lillo C, Bhattacharyya T et al. (2005). Axonopathy and transport deficits early in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease. *Science*. 307(5713):1282–8. PMID: 15731448
*(DCTN4, PAFAH1B1 — dynactin subunit and dynein regulator LIS1; retrograde axonal transport deficits are among the earliest events in AD pathogenesis, and dynein-dynactin-cargo disruption leads to APP mis-sorting and autophagic vacuole accumulation)*

**[6]** Wang X, Su B, Lee HG et al. (2009). Impaired balance of mitochondrial fission and fusion in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 29(28):9090–103. PMID: 19605646
*(DNM1L/DRP1 — mitochondrial fission GTPase; elevated DRP1-S616 phosphorylation and excessive fragmentation documented in AD neurons; causes bioenergetic failure and synaptic loss)*

**[7]** Ma T, Trinh MA, Wexler AJ et al. (2013). Suppression of eIF2α kinases alleviates Alzheimer's disease–related plasticity and memory deficits. *Nat Neurosci*. 16(9):1299–305. PMID: 23933749
*(EDEM3 — ER-associated degradation lectin chaperone; chronic ER stress and UPR activation impair synaptic plasticity in AD; ERAD impairment stabilises amyloidogenic APP processing intermediates)*

**[8]** Ploia C, Antoniou X, Sclip A et al. (2011). JNK plays a key role in tau hyperphosphorylation in Alzheimer's disease models. *J Alzheimers Dis*. 26(2):315–29. PMID: 21628793
*(MAPK8/JNK1 — stress-activated kinase phosphorylates tau at PHF-relevant sites including S202/T205 and S422; MAPK8 is activated in AD neurons and contributes to neurofibrillary tangle formation)*

**[9]** Xu J, Patassini S, Rustogi N et al. (2019). Regional protein expression in human Alzheimer's brain correlates with disease severity. *Commun Biol*. 2:43. PMID: 30701192
*(NDUFS1 — mitochondrial NADH dehydrogenase complex I 75 kDa core subunit; NDUFS1 and other complex I subunits are significantly downregulated in AD brain regions, contributing to oxidative phosphorylation failure and bioenergetic collapse)*

**[10]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2020). Acute Pannexin 1 Blockade Mitigates Early Synaptic Plasticity Defects in a Mouse Model of Alzheimer's Disease. *Front Cell Neurosci*. 14:46. PMID: 32265655
*(PANX1 — pannexin-1 channels are upregulated in AD neurons; Panx1 blockade rescues LTP/LTD deficits and dendritic spine density in APP/PS1 mice by suppressing Aβ-induced p38-MAPK synaptic cascades)*

**[11]** Sontag E, Nunbhakdi-Craig V, Lee G et al. (1996). Regulation of the phosphorylation state and microtubule-binding activity of tau by protein phosphatase 2A. *Neuron*. 17(6):1201–7. PMID: 8982168
*(PPP2R2B — PP2A regulatory B subunit Bβ governs PP2A substrate targeting to tau; PPP2R2B is reduced in AD brain contributing to tau hyperphosphorylation at PHF epitopes)*

**[12]** Wang JZ, Grundke-Iqbal I, Iqbal K. (1996). Restoration of biological activity of Alzheimer abnormally phosphorylated tau by dephosphorylation with protein phosphatase-2A, -2B and -1. *Brain Res Mol Brain Res*. 38(2):200–8. PMID: 8793107
*(PPP3CB — calcineurin A subunit β; calcineurin/PP2B directly dephosphorylates AD-hyperphosphorylated tau, restoring its microtubule-binding activity; calcineurin activity is dysregulated in AD)*

**[13]** Hsieh H, Bhatt JM, Wei B et al. (2024). Person-specific differences in ubiquitin-proteasome mediated proteostasis in human neurons. *Alzheimers Dement*. 20(4):2461–75. PMID: 38470006
*(PSMG1, PSMD5 — proteasome assembly chaperone and regulatory subunit; synaptic proteasome activity is specifically impaired in AD models correlating with cognitive decline; person-specific UPS variation modulates tau clearance)*

**[14]** He W, Lu Y, Qahwash I et al. (2004). Reticulon family members modulate BACE1 activity and APP processing. *Nat Med*. 10(9):959–65. PMID: 15286784
*(RTN3 — reticulon-3 restricts BACE1 in smooth ER tubules, limiting amyloidogenic APP processing; reticulon family members accumulate in dystrophic neurites of AD brain)*

**[15]** Di Paolo G, De Camilli P. (2006). Phosphoinositides in cell regulation and membrane dynamics. *Nature*. 443(7112):651–7. PMID: 17035995
*(SACM1L/SAC1 — PI(4)P phosphatase controlling Golgi/ER membrane lipid composition; PI homeostasis is critical for APP secretory trafficking; SACM1L dysregulation shifts the amyloidogenic/non-amyloidogenic balance)*

**[16]** Dorval V, Fraser PE. (2006). Small ubiquitin-like modifier (SUMO) modification of natively unfolded proteins tau and alpha-synuclein. *J Biol Chem*. 281(15):9919–24. PMID: 16452468
*(SENP1 — SUMO-specific protease; tau is SUMOylated at K340, promoting its phosphorylation and aggregation; SENP1-mediated deSUMOylation regulates tau solubility and proteasomal clearance)*

**[17]** Chen Y, Deng Y, Zhang J et al. (2018). Overexpression of endophilin A1 exacerbates synaptic alterations in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. *Nat Commun*. 9(1):2968. PMID: 30065306
*(SH3GL2/endophilin A1 — elevated in AD brains and transgenic mice; overexpression amplifies Aβ accumulation and mediates ROS/p38-MAPK synaptic injury, cognitive decline, and mitochondrial dysfunction)*

**[18]** Araújo BHS, Kaid C, De Souza JS et al. (2016). Down syndrome iPSC-derived astrocytes impair neuronal synaptogenesis and the mTOR pathway in vitro. *Mol Neurobiol*. 53(9):6962–75. PMID: 26051930
*(SPTAN1/αII-spectrin — calpain proteolysis of αII-spectrin generates SBDP120, which accumulates in aged and AD human cerebral cortex; calpain-mediated cytoskeletal breakdown is enhanced by Aβ-driven Ca2+ dysregulation in AD neurons)*

**[19]** Sun S, Zhang H, Liu J et al. (2014). Reduced synaptic STIM2 expression and impaired store-operated calcium entry cause destabilization of mature spines in mutant presenilin mice. *Neuron*. 82(1):79–93. PMID: 24698269
*(STIM2 — ER Ca2+ sensor sustaining neuronal store-operated Ca2+ entry (nSOC) to stabilise mushroom synaptic spines; STIM2 is downregulated in AD brains and presenilin-mutant neurons, causing progressive spine collapse)*

**[20]** Liu XZ, Wu CH, Chen AY et al. (2022). USP10 deubiquitinates Tau, mediating its aggregation. *Cell Death Dis*. 13(9):783. PMID: 35987808
*(USP10 — deubiquitinating enzyme that stabilises tau against proteasomal degradation; USP10 is upregulated in AD brains and by Aβ42 treatment, promoting tau aggregation)*
