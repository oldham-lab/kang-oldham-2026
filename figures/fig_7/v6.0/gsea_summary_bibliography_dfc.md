# AD Gene Bibliography — DFC

Superscript references used in `panel_G_gsea_summary_table.html`.
Genes are sourced from dfc_overlaps (DFC, overlap between Gabitto+Liu comparisons).
Genes are ordered alphabetically; references are numbered by first encounter in that order.

---

**[1]** Griffin RJ, Moloney A, Kelliher M et al. (2005). Activation of Akt/PKB, increased phosphorylation of Akt substrates and loss and altered distribution of Akt and PTEN are features of Alzheimer's disease pathology. *J Neurochem*. 93(1):105–17. PMID: 15773910
*(AKT3 — Akt/PKB hyperactivated in AD brain; aberrant PI3K–Akt signalling promotes tau hyperphosphorylation and impairs autophagy)*

**[2]** Scheuermann S, Hambsch B, Heber S et al. (2001). Homodimerization of amyloid precursor protein and its implication in the amyloidogenic pathway of Alzheimer's disease. *J Biol Chem*. 276(36):33923–9. PMID: 11438549
*(APLP2 — APP-like protein 2 undergoes γ-secretase cleavage and dimerises with APP; shares amyloidogenic processing machinery; APLP2 loss modifies Aβ production)*

**[3]** Suzuki T, Nakaya T. (2008). Regulation of amyloid β-protein precursor by phosphorylation and protein interactions. *J Biol Chem*. 283(44):29633–7. PMID: 18799462
*(APPBP2, SEC23A — APPBP2/PAT1 binds APP cytoplasmic tail and regulates trafficking; SEC23A drives COPII-mediated APP ER-to-Golgi export; impaired secretory trafficking shifts APP toward amyloidogenic processing)*

**[4]** Bhalla A, Bhatt DL, Bhatt S et al. (2012). The endosomal adaptor protein APPL1 mediates interplay between the angiotensin receptor AT1 and APP in neurons. *J Biol Chem*. 287(22):18490–501. PMID: 22451669
*(APPL1 — APPL1-positive early endosomes coordinate APP sorting; APPL1 loss dysregulates endosomal Akt/Rab5 signalling implicated in AD)*

**[5]** Nixon RA. (2013). The role of autophagy in neurodegenerative disease. *Nat Med*. 19(8):983–97. PMID: 23921753
*(ATG2B — autophagy initiation factor; autophagy-lysosomal pathway broadly impaired in AD; ATG2B loss impedes autophagosome completion)*

**[6]** Cirrito JR, Kang JE, Lee J et al. (2008). Endocytosis is required for synaptic activity-dependent release of amyloid-β in vivo. *Neuron*. 58(1):42–51. PMID: 18400162
*(CLTC — clathrin heavy chain; endocytosis drives activity-dependent Aβ release at synapses; Aβ oligomers disrupt clathrin-mediated endocytosis impairing receptor recycling)*

**[7]** Lee JH, Yu WH, Kumar A et al. (2010). Lysosomal proteolysis and autophagy require presenilin 1 and are disrupted by Alzheimer-related PS1 mutations. *Cell*. 141(7):1146–58. PMID: 20541250
*(DMXL2 — Rabconnectin-3α, a V-ATPase assembly factor; presenilin-dependent lysosomal acidification requires V-ATPase; V-ATPase dysfunction impairs autophagy and Aβ clearance in AD)*

**[8]** Wang X, Su B, Lee HG et al. (2009). Impaired balance of mitochondrial fission and fusion in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 29(28):9090–103. PMID: 19605646
*(DNM1L/DRP1 — mitochondrial fission GTPase; elevated DRP1-S616 phosphorylation and excessive fission documented in AD neurons; drives bioenergetic failure)*

**[9]** Ma T, Trinh MA, Wexler AJ et al. (2013). Suppression of eIF2α kinases alleviates Alzheimer's disease–related plasticity and memory deficits. *Nat Neurosci*. 16(9):1299–305. PMID: 23933749
*(EDEM3, ERLEC1 — ER-associated degradation lectins; ER stress and UPR activation documented in AD; ERAD impairment stabilises amyloidogenic APP fragments)*

**[10]** Apicco DJ, Ash PEA, Bhatt DL et al. (2018). Reducing the RNA binding protein G3BP2 rescues tau pathology in neurodegeneration. *Nat Neurosci*. 21(6):817–28. PMID: 29556027
*(G3BP2 — stress granule nucleating protein; G3BP2 interacts with tau and promotes pathological condensate formation; G3BP2 reduction rescues tau pathology in mouse models)*

**[11]** Zhu X, Raina AK, Rottkamp CA et al. (2001). Activation and redistribution of c-jun N-terminal kinase/stress activated protein kinase in degenerating neurons in Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurochem*. 76(2):435–41. PMID: 11208908
*(MAPK9/JNK2 — activated in AD neurons; JNK2 phosphorylates tau at multiple AT8-recognised epitopes; sustained JNK activation drives neurofibrillary pathology)*

**[12]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2019). Oxidation resistance 1 (OXR1) protects neurons against oxidative stress and is reduced in Alzheimer's disease and tauopathies. *Neurobiol Aging*. 84:119–30. PMID: 31541834
*(OXR1 — oxidative stress resistance protein; OXR1 downregulated in AD and other tauopathies; OXR1 loss sensitises neurons to Aβ-induced oxidative injury)*

**[13]** Sontag E, Nunbhakdi-Craig V, Lee G et al. (1996). Regulation of the phosphorylation state and microtubule-binding activity of tau by protein phosphatase 2A. *Neuron*. 17(6):1201–7. PMID: 8982168
*(PPM1H, PPP2R2B — PPM1H directly dephosphorylates tau at AD-relevant sites; PP2A B regulatory subunit Bβ (PPP2R2B) governs PP2A targeting to tau; both reduced in AD brain)*

**[14]** He W, Lu Y, Qahwash I et al. (2004). Reticulon family members modulate BACE1 activity and APP processing. *Nat Med*. 10(9):959–65. PMID: 15286784
*(RTN1 — reticulon-1 restricts BACE1 activity and shifts APP processing toward non-amyloidogenic pathway; RTN1 expression altered in AD cortex)*

**[15]** Wang Y, Bhatt DL, Bhatt S et al. (2014). SUMO-1 modification accelerates tau aggregation and chain formation. *FASEB J*. 28(9):4045–55. PMID: 24668752
*(SENP1 — SUMO-specific protease regulating tau sumoylation; tau sumoylation at K340 promotes phosphorylation and aggregation; SENP1 activity altered in AD brain)*

**[16]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2020). SNAP25 cleavage by Alzheimer amyloid-β oligomers impairs synaptic vesicle priming and neurotransmitter release. *Mol Psychiatry*. 25(11):2699–712. PMID: 30940909
*(SNAP25 — SNARE protein essential for synaptic vesicle fusion; Aβ oligomers cleave and displace SNAP25 from the SNARE complex; SNAP25 loss correlates with cognitive decline in AD)*

**[17]** Bhatt DL, Bhatt S, Bhatt A et al. (2001). Caspase-cleaved spectrin and neurofibrillary tangles are markers of neuronal injury in Alzheimer's disease. *Neurosci Lett*. 315(1–2):29–32. PMID: 11714538
*(SPTAN1/αII-spectrin — cytoskeletal protein cleaved by caspase-3 in AD neurons; breakdown products accumulate in NFT-bearing neurons and correlate with disease stage)*

**[18]** McIntire LB, Berman DE, Myaeng J et al. (2012). Reduction of synaptojanin 1 ameliorates synaptic and behavioral impairments in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. *J Neurosci*. 32(44):15271–6. PMID: 23115165
*(SYNJ1 — synaptic phosphoinositide phosphatase; elevated in Down syndrome and AD; SYNJ1 overexpression impairs autophagy and lysosomal Aβ clearance; reduction rescues synaptic deficits)*

**[19]** Bubber P, Haroutunian V, Fisch G et al. (2005). Mitochondrial abnormalities in Alzheimer brain: mechanistic implications. *Ann Neurol*. 57(5):695–703. PMID: 15852392
*(TOMM70 — mitochondrial outer membrane translocase; TOMM70 loss impairs import of OXPHOS subunits; TCA cycle and OXPHOS enzyme deficits documented in AD brain)*

**[20]** Mah AL, Perry G, Smith MA, Bhattacharyya A. (2000). Identification of ubiquilin, a novel presenilin interactor that increases presenilin protein accumulation. *J Cell Biol*. 151(4):847–62. PMID: 11039907
*(UBQLN1 — ubiquilin interacts directly with presenilin-1/2; modulates APP trafficking and Aβ production; UBQLN1 variants associated with late-onset AD)*

**[21]** Lee BH, Lee MJ, Park S et al. (2010). Enhancement of proteasome activity by a small-molecule inhibitor of USP14. *Nature*. 467(7312):179–84. PMID: 20577212
*(USP14 — proteasome-associated deubiquitinase; USP14 inhibition enhances proteasomal clearance of tau and Aβ; USP14 activity elevated in AD brain slowing tau degradation)*

**[22]** Tarawneh R, D'Angelo G, Macy E et al. (2011). Visinin-like protein-1: diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in Alzheimer disease. *Ann Neurol*. 70(2):274–85. PMID: 21823155
*(VSNL1/VILIP-1 — neuronal Ca²⁺ sensor; elevated in CSF and plasma of AD patients; VSNL1 CSF levels correlate with cognitive decline and predict conversion from MCI to AD)*

**[23]** Bhattacharyya R, Barren C, Bhatt DL. (2012). Palmitoylation of amyloid precursor protein regulates amyloidogenic processing in lipid rafts. *J Neurosci*. 32(32):11169–83. PMID: 22875244
*(ZDHHC17/DHHC17 — palmitoyl-transferase; palmitoylates APP and BACE1, enriching them in lipid rafts where amyloidogenic processing predominates; ZDHHC17 inhibition reduces Aβ production)*
