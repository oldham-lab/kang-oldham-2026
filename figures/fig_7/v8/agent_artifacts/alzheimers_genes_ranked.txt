Ranked Alzheimer’s Disease Genes (by strength of evidence)

Tier 1 — Definitive / Causal (highest confidence)
1. APOE (ε4 allele)
   - Strongest genetic risk factor for late-onset AD
   - Dose-dependent effect (ε4/ε4 >> ε3/ε4)
   - Replicated across all major cohorts

2. PSEN1 (Presenilin 1)
   - Most common cause of autosomal-dominant early-onset AD
   - Near-complete penetrance

3. APP (Amyloid precursor protein)
   - Mutations and duplications cause familial AD
   - Central to amyloid hypothesis

4. PSEN2 (Presenilin 2)
   - Less common than PSEN1 but clearly causal


Tier 2 — Strong risk genes (rare variants, large effects)
5. TREM2
   - Rare variants (e.g., R47H) increase risk ~2–4×

6. SORL1
   - Loss-of-function variants linked to AD

7. ABCA7
   - Both common and rare variants


Tier 3 — Robust GWAS genes (common variants, modest effects)
8. BIN1
9. CLU (Clusterin / APOJ)
10. PICALM
11. CR1
12. CD33
13. MS4A gene cluster (MS4A4A, MS4A6A)


Tier 4 — Additional replicated but lower-effect loci
14. EPHA1
15. INPP5D
16. MEF2C
17. FERMT2
18. HLA-DRB1 / HLA region


Key Takeaways
- APOE dominates late-onset AD genetics
- APP / PSEN1 / PSEN2 are deterministic
- Many genes implicate immune/microglial pathways
- Effect sizes drop after the top genes
