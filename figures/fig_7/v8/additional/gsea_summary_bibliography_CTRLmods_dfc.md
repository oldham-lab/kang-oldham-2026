# Bibliography: AD gene database — CTRLmods_dfc overlaps
# Genes covered: ATG7, DAPK1, DNM1L, KIF5A, KIF5C, LRRK2, MAPK8, NDUFS1,
#                NFKB1, OPA1, PPP3CB, PSMD5, PSMD11, SORL1, STIM2, SYNJ1,
#                UBQLN1, VPS35

**[1]** Komatsu M et al. (2007). Essential role for autophagy protein Atg7 in the maintenance of axonal homeostasis and the prevention of axonal degeneration. Proc Natl Acad Sci. 104(36):14489-14494. PMID: 17726112
*(ATG7 — neuron-specific deletion of ATG7 in mice causes axonal swellings, ubiquitinated protein accumulation, and progressive neurodegeneration resembling AD pathology, establishing constitutive autophagy as essential for neuronal homeostasis)*

**[2]** Zalckvar E et al. (2009). DAP-kinase-mediated phosphorylation on the BH3 domain of beclin 1 promotes dissociation of beclin 1 from Bcl-XL and induction of autophagy. EMBO Rep. 10(3):285-292. PMID: 19180116
*(DAPK1 — DAPK1 phosphorylates beclin-1 at Thr119 to release it from Bcl-XL and activate autophagy; DAPK1 is also elevated in AD brain and phosphorylates NR2B to mediate Aβ-induced excitotoxicity)*

**[3]** Wang X et al. (2009). Amyloid-beta overproduction causes abnormal mitochondrial dynamics via differential modulation of mitochondrial fission/fusion proteins. J Neurosci. 29(28):9090-9103. PMID: 19605645
*(DNM1L, OPA1 — Aβ overproduction in AD neurons decreases fusion proteins MFN1 and OPA1 while increasing fission mediator DRP1/DNM1L, resulting in mitochondrial fragmentation, bioenergetic failure, and synaptic loss)*

**[4]** Kamal A et al. (2001). Kinesin-mediated axonal transport of a membrane compartment containing β-secretase and GPI-anchored proteins is associated with Alzheimer pathology. Cell. 107(6):787-797. PMID: 11747814
*(KIF5A, KIF5C — conventional kinesin (KIF5 heavy chains) mediates fast anterograde axonal transport of APP together with its processing machinery including BACE1; impaired transport leads to APP retention and enhanced Aβ generation)*

**[5]** Plowey ED et al. (2008). Role of autophagy in G2019S-LRRK2-associated neurite shortening in differentiated SH-SY5Y cells. J Neurochem. 105(3):1048-1056. PMID: 18384652
*(LRRK2 — the AD/PD-relevant kinase LRRK2 phosphorylates multiple autophagy and endolysosomal regulators; pathogenic LRRK2 mutations impair autophagic flux and cause neurite retraction, while LRRK2 also modulates tau phosphorylation via downstream kinase cascades)*

**[6]** Zhu X et al. (2001). Differential activation of neuronal ERK, JNK/SAPK and p38 in Alzheimer disease: the "two hit" hypothesis. Mech Ageing Dev. 123(1):39-46. PMID: 11640950
*(MAPK8 — JNK1/MAPK8 is aberrantly activated in AD neurofibrillary tangle-bearing neurons; JNK phosphorylates tau at Ser202/Thr205 and contributes to progressive tau pathology and neuronal death in AD brain)*

**[7]** Bubber P et al. (2005). Mitochondrial abnormalities in Alzheimer brain: mechanistic implications. Ann Neurol. 57(5):695-703. PMID: 15761824
*(NDUFS1 — activity of NADH dehydrogenase (Complex I, containing NDUFS1) is selectively reduced in AD brain regions with highest neurofibrillary tangle burden, linking Complex I dysfunction to bioenergetic failure in AD)*

**[8]** Kaltschmidt B et al. (1997). Transcription factor NF-κB is activated in primary neurons by amyloid β peptides and in neurons surrounding early plaques from patients with Alzheimer disease. Proc Natl Acad Sci. 94(6):2642-2647. PMID: 9122249
*(NFKB1 — NF-κB/NFKB1 is activated in neurons surrounding amyloid plaques in AD brain and drives expression of pro-inflammatory mediators that amplify synaptic toxicity and tau pathology; Aβ oligomers activate NF-κB directly in primary neurons)*

**[9]** Wu HY et al. (2010). Amyloid β induces the morphological neurodegenerative triad of spine loss, dendritic simplification, and neuritic dystrophies through calcineurin activation. J Neurosci. 30(8):2636-2649. PMID: 20164346
*(PPP3CB — Aβ oligomers activate calcineurin (PP2B, whose regulatory subunit is encoded by PPP3CB), which dephosphorylates cofilin and activates downstream pathways leading to dendritic spine loss and synaptic failure characteristic of early AD)*

**[10]** Keller JN et al. (2000). Impaired proteasome function in Alzheimer's disease. J Neurochem. 75(1):436-439. PMID: 10841806
*(PSMD5, PSMD11 — activities of the 19S regulatory particle (PSMD subunits) are significantly reduced in AD hippocampus, impairing recognition and unfolding of ubiquitinated substrates including tau and APP fragments)*

**[11]** Rogaeva E et al. (2007). The neuronal sortilin-related receptor SORL1 is genetically associated with Alzheimer disease. Nat Genet. 39(2):168-177. PMID: 17220890
*(SORL1 — SORL1/SorLA is a neuronal sorting receptor that diverts APP away from amyloidogenic compartments toward retromer-mediated recycling; SORL1 expression is reduced in AD brain and multiple genetic variants in SORL1 confer AD risk)*

**[12]** Zhang H et al. (2015). Neuronal store-operated calcium entry and mushroom spine loss in amyloid precursor protein knock-in mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. J Neurosci. 35(39):13275-13286. PMID: 26424879
*(STIM2 — STIM2, the ER Ca2+ sensor that activates store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE) to maintain resting spine Ca2+, is reduced in AD brain and AD mouse models; STIM2 downregulation causes mushroom spine loss and synaptic dysfunction reversible by SOCE restoration)*

**[13]** Cossec JC et al. (2012). Trisomy for synaptojanin1 in Down syndrome is functionally linked to the enlargement of early endosomes. Hum Mol Genet. 21(14):3156-3172. PMID: 22505588
*(SYNJ1 — triplication of the chromosome-21 gene SYNJ1 in Down syndrome causes excess PI(4,5)P2 at endocytic membranes, enlarges early endosomes, and accelerates APP processing to Aβ, mechanistically linking SYNJ1 dosage to early-onset AD in trisomy 21)*

**[14]** Mah AL et al. (2000). Identification of ubiquilin, a novel presenilin interactor that increases presenilin protein accumulation. J Cell Biol. 151(4):847-862. PMID: 10952998
*(UBQLN1 — ubiquilin-1 directly binds presenilin-1 and -2 and stabilises their expression; UBQLN1 also acts as a UBL-UBA shuttle factor linking ubiquitinated substrates to the proteasome, and its dysfunction impairs both APP/presenilin stability and proteasomal clearance in AD)*

**[15]** Small SA et al. (2005). Model-guided microarray implicates the retromer complex in Alzheimer's disease. Ann Neurol. 58(6):909-919. PMID: 16315276
*(VPS35 — retromer-complex component VPS35 is selectively reduced in the entorhinal cortex of AD patients; retromer deficiency impairs endosome-to-Golgi retrieval of SorLA/SORL1 and thereby increases APP residence time in endosomes where BACE1 is active, increasing Aβ production)*
