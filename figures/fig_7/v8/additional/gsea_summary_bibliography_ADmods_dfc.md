# Bibliography: AD gene database — ADmods_dfc overlaps
# Genes covered: ATG5, BECN1, CHMP2B, DAPK1, DNM1L, DYRK1A, GSK3B, ITPR1,
#                KIF5A, KIF5C, KLC1, MAP3K12, MAPK8, MFN1, MTOR, NDUFS1, NDUFS4,
#                PPP3CB, PSMA5, PSMB2, PSMB4, PSMB7, PSMD5, PSMD13, RAB5A,
#                STIM2, TGM2, UCHL1, VDAC1, VDAC2, WDFY3

**[1]** Hara T et al. (2006). Suppression of basal autophagy in neural cells causes neurodegenerative disease in mice. Nature. 441(7095):885-889. PMID: 16625204
*(ATG5 — conditional neuronal deletion of ATG5 blocks autophagosome formation, causing progressive neurodegeneration with ubiquitinated inclusions, directly implicating core autophagy machinery in neuronal proteostasis relevant to AD)*

**[2]** Pickford F et al. (2008). The autophagy-related protein beclin 1 shows reduced expression in early Alzheimer disease and regulates amyloid β accumulation in mice. J Clin Invest. 118(6):2190-2199. PMID: 18497889
*(BECN1 — beclin-1 is reduced in early AD cortex; heterozygous deletion in mice increases Aβ accumulation and impairs lysosomal clearance of APP C-terminal fragments)*

**[3]** Skibinski G et al. (2005). Mutations in the endosomal ESCRTIII-complex subunit CHMP2B in frontotemporal dementia. Nat Genet. 37(8):806-808. PMID: 16041373
*(CHMP2B — dominant missense mutations in CHMP2B, an ESCRT-III component required for endosomal sorting and autophagosome maturation, cause frontotemporal dementia with tau/ubiquitin pathology sharing features with AD)*

**[4]** Zalckvar E et al. (2009). DAP-kinase-mediated phosphorylation on the BH3 domain of beclin 1 promotes dissociation of beclin 1 from Bcl-XL and induction of autophagy. EMBO Rep. 10(3):285-292. PMID: 19180116
*(DAPK1 — DAPK1 phosphorylates beclin-1 at Thr119 to release it from Bcl-XL and activate autophagy; DAPK1 is also elevated in AD brain and phosphorylates NR2B to mediate Aβ-induced excitotoxicity)*

**[5]** Wang X et al. (2009). Amyloid-beta overproduction causes abnormal mitochondrial dynamics via differential modulation of mitochondrial fission/fusion proteins. J Neurosci. 29(28):9090-9103. PMID: 19605645
*(DNM1L, MFN1 — Aβ overproduction in AD neurons decreases fusion proteins MFN1 and OPA1 while increasing fission mediator DRP1/DNM1L, resulting in mitochondrial fragmentation, bioenergetic failure, and synaptic loss)*

**[6]** Ryoo SR et al. (2007). DYRK1A-mediated hyperphosphorylation of Tau: a functional link between Down syndrome and Alzheimer disease. J Biol Chem. 282(48):34850-34857. PMID: 17906291
*(DYRK1A — DYRK1A directly phosphorylates tau at Thr212, priming it for subsequent GSK3β phosphorylation; DYRK1A overexpression in Down syndrome and AD brain contributes to neurofibrillary tangle formation)*

**[7]** Phiel CJ et al. (2003). GSK-3α regulates production of Alzheimer's disease amyloid-β peptides. Nature. 423(6938):435-439. PMID: 12761548
*(GSK3B — GSK3 isoforms regulate both APP processing to generate Aβ and tau phosphorylation at multiple AD-relevant epitopes; GSK3 inhibition reduces Aβ42 production and tau hyperphosphorylation in vivo)*

**[8]** Stutzmann GE et al. (2004). Dysregulated IP3 signaling in cortical neurons of knock-in mice expressing an Alzheimer's-linked mutation in presenilin1 results in exaggerated Ca2+ signals and altered membrane excitability. Neuron. 42(4):499-510. PMID: 15047074
*(ITPR1 — PS1 FAD mutations potentiate IP3R-mediated ER Ca2+ release, causing supranormal cytosolic Ca2+ transients that contribute to synaptic dysfunction and neurodegeneration in AD)*

**[9]** Kamal A et al. (2001). Kinesin-mediated axonal transport of a membrane compartment containing β-secretase and GPI-anchored proteins is associated with Alzheimer pathology. Cell. 107(6):787-797. PMID: 11747814
*(KIF5A, KIF5C, KLC1 — conventional kinesin (KIF5 heavy chains plus KLC1 light chain) mediates fast anterograde axonal transport of APP together with its processing machinery including BACE1; impaired transport leads to APP retention and enhanced Aβ generation)*

**[10]** Le Pichon CE et al. (2017). Loss of dual leucine zipper kinase signaling is protective in animal models of neurodegenerative disease. Sci Transl Med. 9(403):eaag0394. PMID: 28794283
*(MAP3K12 — MAP3K12/DLK is a critical mediator of the neuronal stress-response kinase cascade activated downstream of Aβ and tau pathology; genetic or pharmacological DLK inhibition reduces synaptic loss and neurodegeneration in AD models)*

**[11]** Zhu X et al. (2001). Differential activation of neuronal ERK, JNK/SAPK and p38 in Alzheimer disease: the "two hit" hypothesis. Mech Ageing Dev. 123(1):39-46. PMID: 11640950
*(MAPK8 — JNK1/MAPK8 is aberrantly activated in AD neurofibrillary tangle-bearing neurons; JNK phosphorylates tau at Ser202/Thr205 and contributes to the progressive tau pathology and neuronal death in AD brain)*

**[12]** Spilman P et al. (2010). Inhibition of mTOR by rapamycin abolishes cognitive deficits and reduces amyloid-beta levels in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. PLoS ONE. 5(4):e9979. PMID: 20376348
*(MTOR — mTOR hyperactivation in AD suppresses autophagy and promotes tau and Aβ accumulation; rapamycin treatment restores autophagy, reduces Aβ42 load, and rescues cognitive deficits in AD mouse models)*

**[13]** Bubber P et al. (2005). Mitochondrial abnormalities in Alzheimer brain: mechanistic implications. Ann Neurol. 57(5):695-703. PMID: 15761824
*(NDUFS1, NDUFS4 — activities of NADH dehydrogenase (Complex I) and other mitochondrial enzyme complexes are selectively reduced in AD brain regions with highest neurofibrillary tangle burden, linking NDUFS1/NDUFS4-containing Complex I to bioenergetic failure in AD)*

**[14]** Wu HY et al. (2010). Amyloid β induces the morphological neurodegenerative triad of spine loss, dendritic simplification, and neuritic dystrophies through calcineurin activation. J Neurosci. 30(8):2636-2649. PMID: 20164346
*(PPP3CB — Aβ oligomers activate calcineurin (PP2B, encoded by PPP3CB regulatory subunit), which dephosphorylates cofilin and activates downstream pathways leading to dendritic spine loss and synaptic failure characteristic of early AD)*

**[15]** Keller JN et al. (2000). Impaired proteasome function in Alzheimer's disease. J Neurochem. 75(1):436-439. PMID: 10841806
*(PSMA5, PSMB2, PSMB4, PSMB7, PSMD5, PSMD13 — chymotrypsin-like, trypsin-like, and peptidyl-glutamyl-peptide hydrolase activities of the 20S proteasome (PSMB subunits) and 19S regulatory particle (PSMD/PSMA subunits) are all significantly reduced in AD hippocampus, impairing clearance of oxidised and ubiquitinated proteins)*

**[16]** Ginsberg SD et al. (2010). Upregulation of select rab GTPases in cholinergic basal forebrain neurons in mild cognitive impairment and Alzheimer's disease. J Alzheimers Dis. 22(3):771-781. PMID: 20847419
*(RAB5A — RAB5A is selectively upregulated in vulnerable cholinergic neurons in MCI and AD, consistent with enlarged early endosomes and increased endocytic flux that accelerates Aβ generation from APP in the endosomal compartment)*

**[17]** Zhang H et al. (2015). Neuronal store-operated calcium entry and mushroom spine loss in amyloid precursor protein knock-in mouse model of Alzheimer's disease. J Neurosci. 35(39):13275-13286. PMID: 26424879
*(STIM2 — STIM2, the ER Ca2+ sensor that activates store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE) to maintain resting spine Ca2+, is reduced in AD brain and AD mouse models; STIM2 downregulation causes mushroom spine loss and synaptic dysfunction reversible by SOCE restoration)*

**[18]** Johnson GVW et al. (1997). Transglutaminase activity is increased in Alzheimer's disease brain. Brain Res. 751(2):323-329. PMID: 9097410
*(TGM2 — transglutaminase 2 activity is elevated in AD neocortex and cross-links tau into SDS-insoluble polymers resembling paired helical filament components, suggesting TGM2-catalysed isopeptide bond formation stabilises neurofibrillary tangle material)*

**[19]** Choi J et al. (2004). Oxidative modifications and down-regulation of ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase L1 associated with idiopathic Parkinson's and Alzheimer's diseases. J Biol Chem. 279(13):13256-13264. PMID: 14722078
*(UCHL1 — UCH-L1/UCHL1 is oxidatively carbonylated and down-regulated in AD brain; loss of UCH-L1 deubiquitinase activity impairs ubiquitin recycling and proteasome-mediated clearance of misfolded proteins including tau and APP fragments)*

**[20]** Anandatheerthavarada HK et al. (2003). Mitochondrial targeting and a novel transmembrane arrest of Alzheimer's amyloid precursor protein impairs mitochondrial function as a new mechanism of neuronal death. J Cell Biol. 161(6):1079-1090. PMID: 14691135
*(VDAC1, VDAC2 — full-length APP accumulates in the mitochondrial import channel where it physically interacts with VDAC1/VDAC2, inhibiting mitochondrial import of cytochrome c oxidase subunits and reducing Complex IV activity and ΔΨm in AD neurons)*

**[21]** Filimonenko M et al. (2010). The selective macroautophagic degradation of aggregated proteins requires the PI3P-binding protein Alfy. Mol Cell. 38(2):265-279. PMID: 20417604
*(WDFY3 — WDFY3/Alfy is the PI3P-binding scaffold that recruits the ATG5-12-16L1 conjugation complex to ubiquitinated protein aggregates for selective macroautophagic clearance; its loss causes accumulation of polyubiquitinated inclusions resembling those in AD and other neurodegenerative diseases)*
